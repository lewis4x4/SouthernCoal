## Executive Summary

Southern Coal's enterprise-wide pain concentrates on failure-to-sample, not water quality: 3,863 missed 2025 sampling events (a 12.7% failure rate across TN/KY/VA/WV) against 213+ NPDES permits, with WV alone accounting for 2,289 misses at a 59.3% submission rate, drove ~$9.665M in 2025 stipulated-penalty exposure that is still recomputed by hand every quarter. The company is simultaneously paralyzed on a ~$1.8M/yr third-party lab decision (Aquatic) versus a ~$1.06M/yr in-house field program, because lab analytical contracts are negotiated in zero states and the $38.49/event break-even cannot be validated until a real lab price exists. Detection is the core structural failure — misses surface one-to-four quarters late, the platform runs no nightly calendar-gap job despite the Consent Decree's tight notice clocks, and Force Majeure defenses have already been at serious risk of forfeiture (no timely 3-business-day notice on record — a legal determination for counsel) on the two largest January 2026 exposures (S402586/$299K, S400311/$8K) because notices were not filed inside the 3-business-day window. Two hard blockers gate whole domains: the Sampling Matrix has not been received (so the obligation set that would generate the calendar is unbuilt), and Tom's MSHA mine-IDs are not loaded (so sync-msha-data returns 503/501 and all 11+ facilities are blind to MSHA data). Beneath the compliance spine, the core operational tables are empty or misscoped — there is no heavy-equipment asset register, no PM-interval engine, no scale-ticket/shipment/demurrage schema across ~93 tables, and no DMLR-to-NPDES crosswalk for 42+ VA permits (where the AI parser fabricated NPDES-looking IDs) — so production, transport, fleet, and reclamation are largely invisible to the platform officers rely on. The through-line is that legal liability is real and accruing while the detection, evidence, and reconciliation infrastructure that would contain it does not yet exist.

## The Five Sharpest Pains Across the Enterprise

1. **No real-time missed-sampling detection** (Environmental Compliance, rank 1) — Misses are found 1–4 quarters late with no nightly calendar-gap job despite the ¶49 posture, so the single largest litigation exposure grows silently before anyone can resample or mount a defense. This is top-tier because failure-to-sample is *the* Consent-Decree liability and the detection loop is the highest-leverage fix in the entire mesh.

2. **Force Majeure clocks silently expiring** (Legal / Risk / Audit, rank 2) — FM notices for January 2026 events were not filed inside the 3-business-day window, putting the defense on the two largest exposures (S402586/$299K, S400311/$8K) at serious risk of forfeiture (a determination for counsel), with no evidence any notice was filed. It is top-tier because a missed clock converts a defensible miss into an unchallengeable penalty and directly implicates Steve Ball's certification-under-penalty-of-law exposure.

3. **The $1.8M make-vs-buy decision is frozen** (Financial & Contracts, rank 3) — Lab analytical contracts are negotiated in zero states, so the $38.49/event break-even between the ~$1.8M Aquatic contract and the ~$1.06M in-house program cannot be validated and the whole capital decision is stuck. It is top-tier because it is the largest recurring dollar lever in the business and it gates the field-staffing program that would fix pain #1.

4. **Contradictory litigation records** (Legal / Risk / Audit, rank 2) — All 161 of Gargon's missed records read "NO ACCESS" in lab data while the separately-prepared FTS sheet says "ROAD CONDITIONS," an after-the-fact edit with no source document that, if compared by opposing counsel, undermines the credibility of *both* evidentiary records. It is top-tier because document-integrity failures poison the entire defense, not just one line item, under federal oversight.

5. **Two blockers gate whole domains** (Environmental Compliance + Mine Safety, ranks 1 & 6) — The Sampling Matrix has not been received (so no live per-outfall/per-parameter obligation calendar can be generated) and Tom's MSHA mine-IDs are not loaded (so sync-msha-data returns 503/501 and all 11+ facilities are MSHA-blind). These are top-tier because they are single, human-supplied inputs that unlock the compliance calendar and the entire mine-safety picture at once.

## Method & Scoring

Eleven enterprise domains were mapped, each grounded in real source artifacts (meeting recaps, the Consent Decree compliance analysis, the January 2026 concerns memo, the function inventory, ECHO sync report, and direct inspection of the Supabase migrations/functions) rather than assumption. Each domain was scored on five axes, 1–5: **legal** exposure, **dollar** exposure, **frequency**, **people** affected, and **automatability**. Composite rank uses a fixed weighting of **legal .30 / dollar .25 / frequency .15 / people .15 / automatability .15**. Legal and dollar lead the weighting deliberately: the company operates under an active Clean Water Act Consent Decree (Case 7:16-cv-00462-GEC) where a single missed clock or uncertified filing carries criminal certification risk and per-event stipulated penalties, so the axes that map to that exposure must dominate ranking. Where data does not yet exist — empty production/transport/fleet tables, un-received Sampling Matrix, unloaded MSHA mine-IDs, missing DMLR crosswalk — the domain is explicitly flagged as a GAP or a build-the-pipe-first area; no tonnage, penalty, or asset numbers were invented to fill those holes.

### 1. Environmental Compliance (Module 1 — built, extend it)

This is the built spine and the core of the Consent Decree — the richest and highest-stakes of the eleven domains. Everything below extends it; nothing here should be rebuilt.

Results become HMRs/DMRs keyed into five state portals on quarterly deadlines (Apr 30 / Jul 31 / Oct 31 / Jan 31). CD Paragraph 49 (effective 2016-10-30) requires labs transmit *all* DMR results within 48 hours of analysis. The defining truth, stated bluntly in the Feb 21 meeting, is that **the failure is failure-to-SAMPLE, not water quality**: of 30,412 required 2025 events, 26,549 were submitted (87.3%), leaving **3,863 missed events** — while water quality on samples actually taken runs ~99.9% compliant (`SCC_Meeting_Recap_Feb21.pdf §1-2`).

**Break points.** (1) **No real-time detection** — misses are found 1-4 quarters late; the 24-hour alerting the meeting called a "long-term goal" is unbuilt, and no calendar-gap detection job exists in the codebase. (2) **The Sampling Matrix blocker** — the master outfall→parameter→frequency→lab→method→responsible-party map has not been received, so `sampling_calendar`/`sampling_schedules` cannot be seeded and the 30,412 baseline itself "may be inflated; WV permits may have lapsed" (`§8`). (3) **Empty core tables** — `npdes_permits/outfalls/permit_limits/sampling_events/lab_results/dmr_submissions/exceedances` are effectively empty pending the Upload Dashboard, which is why all **144,339 ECHO discrepancies fire as Rule 2 'missing_internal'** with nothing internal to compare against (`ECHO_SYNC_REPORT.md`). (4) **Lab ¶49 gap** — Aquatic historically sent exceedance-only data, not complete EDDs (potential 9+ year violation), and gave **zero reason codes for Q1-Q3 2025** (75.7% of misses undocumented). (5) **WV is worst at 59.3% submission**, with Aquatic possibly subcontracting the fieldwork (`§5`). (6) **Alabama is 100% paper** with zero ingestion and is excluded from every total. (7) **42 VA permits use DMLR mining IDs, not federal NPDES**, so they cannot ECHO-sync and VA exceedance detection is impossible; VA/TN lab parsers are BLOCKED on sample files (`DATA_REQUEST_TN_VA.md`).

**Who feels it.** Steve Ball (EVP & GC) is the liable officer — he certifies quarterly compliance under penalty of law and confirmed the company bears ultimate CD responsibility "regardless of lab performance." Tom Lusk (COO) owns permit-list accuracy and field staffing; Jon Lawson already carries the field burden of disproving Aquatic's locked-gate claims in VA.

### 2. Legal / Risk / Audit

**Today-state.** This is the cross-cutting accountability layer over the entire Consent Decree (*US v. Southern Coal Corp.*, 7:16-cv-00462-GEC — 839 pages, **75 tracked obligations**, oversight by EPA Regions III/IV, DOJ ENRD, OSMRE, and five state DEPs). General Counsel **Steve Ball** manages it largely by hand. The CD deliberately has **no single master deadline schedule** — each obligation runs on its own clock (fixed dates, recurring cycles, event-triggered clocks, retroactive post-lodging enforcement) and the government structured it so that *administrative* failure (late notice, missing log, uncertified report) is penalized as harshly as an actual discharge (`SCC_Consent_Decree_Executive_Handoff.md §4,§7`). It is a **court order, not a settlement**: violations expose the company to stipulated penalties, non-capped statutory penalties, injunctive relief, and contempt, and paying does not excuse performance (`SCC_Consent_Decree_Legal_Counsel_Brief.md §1`). Quarterly reports and DMRs are **certified under penalty of law**, carrying criminal exposure (`Executive_Handoff §6`).

**Break points.** The January 2026 file is the sharpest evidence of where this breaks:
- **Force Majeure defenses forfeited by inaction** — no 3-business-day notice appears to have been filed for the two largest exposures (S402586/Nufac **$299,000**; S400311 **$8,000**), which appears to forfeit the only viable defense — a determination for counsel (`January2026_Concerns_v2.pdf C-3, Q-1, Q-2`).
- **Contradictory litigation records** — all 161 of Gargon's missed records read **"NO ACCESS"** in lab data, while the separately-prepared FTS sheet says **"ROAD CONDITIONS"**, added after the fact with no source document; comparing them undermines the credibility of *both* (`C-2, Q-3`).
- **A hand-maintained legal artifact that is provably wrong** — the FTS payment sheet totals **$1,060,000** but is understated by **≥$8,000**, with three permit entries missing entirely (`C-1, C-6, Q-9`).
- **$126,000 in fines on 4 permits not in the authoritative active-operations list** — validity and challengeability unresolved, with no workflow reconciling fines against active status before the Apr 30 payment (`C-7–C-9, Q-4`).
- Beyond January: penalty **payment status is unknown** (paid? disputed? accruing interest?), there is **no regulatory-correspondence log, legal-matter register, or privilege register** as a system of record, and the **quarterly certification has no defined authorized signatory or escalation** (`Executive_Handoff §5E`; grounding brief Domain 2). *GAP — exact CD Appendix escalators and the per-state federal/state penalty split are not in sources (owner: Steve Ball).*

**Who feels it.** Steve Ball (GC) carries the certification, privilege, FM-notice, and litigation-hold exposure directly; outside counsel Steven R. Ruby (Carey Douglas Kessler & Ruby) inherits any weakened evidentiary posture; Jay Justice (owner) bears ultimate CD responsibility; Tom Lusk and Bill Johnson feed the operational and data facts that become the defense.

**What the brain changes.** The Sovereign Brain becomes the single **immutable clock and evidence spine** for all 75 obligations: it senses every clock-starting event and computes the notice deadline (24-hr oral, 5-day written, 3-business-day FM) and the days-at-risk penalty tier, escalating **before** the Day-15/Day-31 jumps; it refuses to let a Force Majeure clock expire silently, opening an FM matter with a countdown that can't close without a filed notice + best-efforts evidence (this alone flags the $299K forfeiture risk in real time); it cross-checks every fine against the active-operations list; it refuses to certify a report whose underlying records contradict; and it maintains a privilege/CBI-aware correspondence and matter register. This domain is the **local-only (Hermes) core** — only sanitized aggregates cross the audited boundary.

**Platform spine today (real, but empty of the rows it governs).** Unusually well-built for this project. ### 4. Quality Control & Customer Specs

**Today-state.** QC at Justice/SCC is really two domains wearing one name, and the sources treat them very differently.
