The way to think about it
A coal mine is not one business. It is a production company, trucking/logistics company, environmental compliance company, safety-regulated workforce company, heavy-equipment maintenance company, land/mineral-rights company, finance/royalty company, and legal-risk company all stacked on top of each other.
For SCC/Justice specifically, your files already frame this correctly: the platform is for coal mining across AL, KY, TN, VA, and WV, under EPA, MSHA, OSMRE, and state DEP oversight, with legal-compliance data requiring immutable audit trails. The Consent Decree definition of a “Facility” is broad enough to include surface mines, underground mines, preparation plants, refuse facilities, coal transportation facilities, reclamation areas, and associated surface coal mining/reclamation operations. SCC’s EMS Manual also says the system covers exploration, mining, processing, transportation, maintenance, and administration.

For WV, VA, and KY, the system needs to track both mine-side operations and state/federal compliance. OSMRE explains that SMCRA governs environmentally responsible coal mining and reclamation, with primacy states handling permitting, inspection, and enforcement while OSMRE provides oversight. WVDEP says its Division of Mining and Reclamation oversees surface and underground coal mines, preparation plants, coal loading facilities, haulage ways, coal-related dams, and mining NPDES permits. Kentucky’s Division of Mine Permits reviews mining/reclamation plans and issues permits under SMCRA performance standards, and Kentucky’s KPDES program governs discharges to waters of the Commonwealth. Virginia Energy’s coal mine licensing process covers new mines, renewals, changes of ownership, tax affidavits, tonnage forms, final maps, and operator-change notices.

Your force majeure guide says it is the only mechanism under the Consent Decree that can excuse a missed sample, and it requires a 3-business-day initial notice plus a full written explanation within 7 calendar days, with supporting documentation. The Consent Decree also places the burden on Defendants to prove the delay was caused by Force Majeure, that best efforts were used, and that the notice requirements were met.

25. The non-negotiable human review points
Even with agents, humans must remain in charge of these decisions:
Declaring or denying force majeure
Legal interpretation of Consent Decree obligations
Certifying DMRs and quarterly reports
Approving permit applications/modifications/transfers
Changing sampling schedules
Closing major corrective actions
Abating MSHA citations
Contesting citations/orders
Approving capital spend
Hiring/firing key operational personnel
Suspending mining due to safety risk
Releasing a mine or area back to production after a serious incident
Approving bond release packages
Signing regulatory submissions
Making public/CBI/privilege determinations
Approving customer claim settlements
Approving mine closure or idling
The system should make these decisions hard to miss, easy to evidence, and impossible to quietly bury.
Bottom line
The opportunity is bigger than “water sampling app.” The real product is a Coal Mine Operating & Compliance Command Center for WV, VA, and KY that treats every mine, permit, outfall, employee, contractor, truck, sample, lab result, citation, inspection, invoice, and legal deadline as part of one connected operating graph.
That graph should answer four questions every day:

Are we allowed to operate?
Are we operating safely?
Are we meeting every environmental/legal obligation?
Are we making money without creating hidden legal exposure?
